# SIH26166 Ground Truth Validation Report

## 1. What are the sources of your reference data?
The primary source of geographic metadata is the IIT InterIIT Tech Meet 11.0 repository, which contains corner coordinates scraped from the ISRO PRADAN portal. A secondary source is the USGS / Ames Stereo Pipeline documentation. Independent verification data (LOLA DEM) is available via the PDS Geosciences Node (Imbrium).

## 2. Are they derived from the same mission (Chandrayaan-2)?
Yes, 19 of the 20 pairs use reference metadata derived entirely from Chandrayaan-2 (OHRC and TMC-2). One pair (pair_012) incorporates independent data from the LRO mission (LOLA).

## 3. Did you include independent references like NASA LOLA or Kaguya TC?
Yes, pair_012 is a south-polar region at ~20°E where USGS/ASP documented a comparison between Chandrayaan-2 and NASA LOLA. It is labeled as `GEOREFERENCED_REFERENCE`.

## 4. Are your "control points" manually verified pixel correspondences?
No. Zero control points were manually verified.

## 5. Or are they just geographic mappings from one image to another?
They are purely geographic mappings. All 612 control points are labeled `geospatially_derived`. They were generated by taking a geographic grid within the overlap region and bilinearly interpolating the coordinates back into the image pixel space based on corner metadata.

## 6. How many exact pixel-level GT points do you have?
Zero on real images. We have exact pixel-level ground truth on 5 *synthetic* examples (rotations, translations, scaling) located in `validation/synthetic/`.

## 7. How did you verify the scenes actually overlap?
By mathematically intersecting the geographic bounding boxes defined by the corner coordinates. For 16 pairs, the overlap is confirmed by the IIT team's dataset. For 4 pairs, the overlap is inferred but unconfirmed. The generated 6×6 geographic grids exist for 17 pairs; pairs 003, 004, and 015 have no grid because the current reference-footprint model yields no geographic intersection.

## 8. Did you download the actual image data?
No. The actual image pixels remain behind the ISRO PRADAN login portal. The current dataset contains the directory structure, metadata, and derivable geographic mappings.

## 9. Is there sufficient scene diversity?
Yes. The 20 pairs cover:
- South Polar craters (41°E, 43°E, 20°E, 341°E)
- Southern Highlands (56°E, 39°E, 31°E)
- Mid-South Plains (41°E, 25°E)
- Equatorial South (70°E)

## 10. How will you evaluate sub-pixel accuracy without sub-pixel ground truth?
Currently, we cannot. Sub-pixel validation will require either: a) Manual sub-pixel annotation by the CV team on a subset of points, or b) Using the synthetic transformations dataset where the exact sub-pixel mapping is mathematically known.

## 11. Are there duplicate scenes in your dataset?
No. Duplicate products from the same orbit pass (e.g., `d32` vs `d18` versions of the same footprint) were explicitly rejected and documented in `rejected.csv`.

## 12. Are there exactly 15–20 usable pairs?
Yes. There are 20 pairs. 16 are labeled Quality B (useful, overlap confirmed) and 4 are labeled Quality C (inferred overlap).

## 13. What is your strongest scientifically documented pair?
Pair 012 (`ch2_ohr_nrp_20200827T0226453039_d_img_d18` and `ch2_tmc_ndn_20231101T0125121377_d_oth_d18`). It is explicitly documented in the USGS Ames Stereo Pipeline tutorials and compared against LOLA.

## 14. What happens if the CV pipeline fails on this dataset?
If it fails on the real images, it could be due to: extreme illumination differences, scale differences (0.25m vs 5m), or inaccuracies in the corner coordinate interpolation. If it fails on the `synthetic/` dataset, the pipeline itself has a bug.

## 15. Is this dataset ready for the CV team?
Yes, for preliminary development. The metadata, pairs, and scripts are established. The CV team must first download the raw `.img` files via PRADAN to proceed with image processing.
